05

The second problem: the wrong goal is set

Goal: Maximise installs
$2.36
Cost per result · "Women Campaign for sign ups" (matchcatch.com)
70×more expensive
Goal: Confirmed female sign-up
$164.78
Cost per result · ad set "Sign_Up | Women | Singapore" (matchcatch.com), only 2 results
  • The campaign "Women Campaign for sign ups" runs on the matchcatch.com account. It is actually optimised for installs: its goal is set to "Maximise number of app installs," which ignores gender completely, even though the campaign name is about sign-ups.
  • Meta cannot tell if an install came from a woman. It only looks for the cheapest install, from anyone. When the ad set is switched to count only confirmed female sign-ups, the cost jumps 70×, because most of the audience being reached is already male.
  • Put together: ads going to a broad audience, plus a goal that ignores gender, always ends up favouring men, because men are cheaper to reach right now.

  • The measured cost per female install is $6.09 on matchcatch.com and $6.74 on MatchCatch US Shadan.
  • That is lower than the $30–40 SGD figure you flagged. The gap likely comes from two things: money spent reaching men by mistake (shown above), and the normal drop-off between an install and a completed, verified sign-up (face verification, and in Singapore, the new age check).
  • Fixing the targeting removes the biggest part of the gap, and the part you can control directly.
Worth confirming on the call: is your $30–40 SGD figure cost per install, or cost per completed sign-up? The $6.09–$6.74 numbers above are cost per install. If your figure is measured at completed sign-up, both numbers could be correct at the same time, they would just be measuring two different steps of the funnel.
